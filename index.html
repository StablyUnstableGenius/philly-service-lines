Replacement programs

PWD replaces lead and galvanized lines at no cost in two situations: on blocks receiving a new water main, which covers about 1% of city mains per year, and, from August 2026, through a pilot in ten North and West Philadelphia ZIP codes budgeted for about 1,000 lines. Other owners can finance replacement with a zero-interest loan.

Options for property owners

  • Check the address on PWD's Service Line Map.
  • Lead line: apply for a HELP loan, or hire a licensed plumber (PWD cites $3,000–$9,000).
  • Lead or galvanized line: pre-apply to the pilot. Outside pilot ZIP codes, this registers interest.
  • Incomplete records: check the pipe at your meter and report the material through PWD's form.

Replacement: ReplaceServiceLine@phila.gov, (215) 683-8825
HELP: PWDHELPloan@phila.gov, (215) 685-4901

Options for renters

  • Check your address on PWD's Service Line Map.
  • Only the owner can approve a replacement. Renters can submit the pilot pre-application with the owner's contact details, which shares the permission form with the owner.
  • Tell your landlord about the HELP loan, which covers tenant-occupied homes of up to four units.
  • Request a free lead test of your tap water from PWD.

PWD's pilot case manager can help contact your landlord: ReplaceServiceLine@phila.gov, (215) 683-8825

Reducing exposure

  • PWD has used corrosion control (zinc orthophosphate) for over 30 years; it reports lead below the EPA standard of 15 ppb in 90% or more of homes.
  • If water has sat unused for 6 hours or more, flush by running the cold kitchen tap for 3–5 minutes.
  • Use only cold water for drinking, cooking and baby formula. Use filters certified for lead reduction (NSF). Boiling does not remove lead.
  • The Health Department recommends blood lead tests for all children aged 1–6 (PWD FAQ).

Data and method

Service line records: PWD public inventory layer (vwPublicFacingInventory), 530,791 records, last edited November 14, 2026; retrieved November 24, 2026. PWD's "Unknown" class is labeled "Incomplete records," PWD's current term. Records were grouped into hexagons about 300 m across and assigned to ZIP codes by location using the City's ZIP code boundaries; 228 records fall outside those boundaries and are excluded from ZIP figures. Hexagons with fewer than 15 lines are shown without a rate. October 2024 figures: PWD press release. Program details: PWD and City pages as of November 2026.
